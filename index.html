Reading this brief

What the numbers mean. Breach times, canal capacities, surge stages, wall-failure elevations, flood extents (~20% by Aug 29 afternoon, ~80% by Aug 30–31, depths to 15–20 ft, equalization with the lake at ≈ +3 ft on Sep 1), shelter counts and rescue totals are drawn from the IPET, ILIT and ASCE investigations, the Congressional “A Failure of Initiative” report, NWS/NOAA records, and contemporaneous reporting. Where the record gives a window (e.g., 17th St failure reported both ~06:30 by IPET and ~09:45 in early accounts), the earlier engineering estimate is used and the window noted in the event pin.

What is modeled. Continuous depth curves between documented checkpoints, the % flooded curve, and the neighborhood split of the stranded population are linear interpolations anchored to documented totals. Street and building fabric is procedural on a topology-faithful schematic — drawn to make breach-to-block inundation legible, not a cadastral survey. Floodwater spreads from documented breach points with street-preferential routing and lagged block fill; depths still track the basin keyframes above.

The core finding, per ASCE: roughly two-thirds of the flooding would not have happened had the floodwalls held. Sheet piles driven to ~17 ft instead of the 31–46 ft the soils required saved about $100M and cost the system its integrity: the 17th St wall failed with water ~5 ft below its crown, London Avenue failed ~4 ft below design stage, and the pumps — built for rainfall, powered from inside the bowl, discharging into the very canals that were failing — never had a move to make.
